Luxury Beauty · The Edit

Prestige at marketplace prices — if the seller checks out.

Designer skincare is genuinely cheaper online than at a department store counter, on a good day. On a bad day, this is the single highest counterfeit-risk category we cover. Here's the one habit that separates the two.

By the Naked Compound Research Team· 5 min read· Updated September 2026

Luxury and prestige beauty is one of the most counterfeited product categories on Indian marketplaces — a fact that has nothing to do with the brands themselves and everything to do with margin. A ₹6,000 serum has far more room for a convincing fake than a ₹200 face wash, which is exactly why it attracts more of them.

The good news: authenticity risk in this category collapses almost entirely to one checkable fact — who the listing is sold by. This edit covers what to check, and three picks that clear the bar.

Do It Yourself

How to check any listing.

Run these four before you check out on anything above ₹2,000.

Seller check

Who it's sold by

Sold directly by the brand or a named, brand-authorised seller — the single highest-value check for this category.

Batch code check

Verifiable, not hidden

A listing photo that clearly shows the batch code, and a brand that lets you verify it, is a good sign. A code that's cropped out or won't verify is not.

Import check

Grey-market awareness

Genuinely imported stock still needs to clear CDSCO import compliance. Listings with no origin information at all warrant a closer look.

Price-per-ml check

Sanity, not suspicion

A price far below every other listing for the same size is more often a different (older, smaller, or grey-market) batch than a deal — check the fine print.

Frequently asked
Is luxury beauty bought online actually authentic?
Only if the listing is sold directly by the brand or by a brand-authorised seller, shown under the "Sold by" line on the product page. Unauthorised third-party sellers are the highest-risk source for counterfeit fragrance and skincare online — check the seller before the price, not after.
Why is designer skincare cheaper online than in a department store?
Online retailers carry lower overhead than counter retail and run frequent promotions, so a genuine price gap exists. The same gap is also what makes this category attractive to counterfeiters and grey-market sellers — the discount itself isn't a red flag, an unverifiable seller is.
What's a batch code and why does it matter?
A batch code is a small printed or embossed code on the product or packaging that brands use to trace manufacturing date and production line. Most luxury brands let you verify a batch code on their official site or via customer care — a code that doesn't verify, or a listing photo that hides it, is worth treating with caution.
Does CDSCO regulate luxury cosmetics differently from mass-market ones?
No. CDSCO's manufacturing and import compliance framework under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act applies the same way regardless of price point. A higher price does not mean higher regulatory scrutiny — authenticity and seller verification matter just as much for a ₹6,000 serum as a ₹300 one.
