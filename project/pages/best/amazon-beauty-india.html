Skin Care on Amazon India

Skincare on marketplaces suffers from a specific, fixable problem: label claims (SPF number, "clinically proven," "10% vitamin C") are copied into the listing title without the supporting detail — PA rating, testing methodology, or actual formulation percentage — that makes the claim meaningful. The fix is reading past the headline claim on every listing.

SPF ≠ PA
SPF and PA measure two different things. SPF quantifies UVB protection (sunburn risk); PA quantifies UVA protection (pigmentation, photoageing) on the JCIA PA+ to PA++++ scale most Indian sunscreen brands now use. A listing that shows only SPF, with no PA rating anywhere in the title, bullet points, or product images, is giving incomplete sun-protection information — worth checking before you buy, especially for daily wear in Indian UV conditions.

Why label literacy matters here

How we score →

Cosmetics in India are regulated under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act via CDSCO — a manufacturing and import compliance framework, not a clinical-efficacy gatekeeper the way FSSAI functions for food supplements. That means a "10% vitamin C" or "clinically proven" claim on a skincare listing can be technically legal while telling you almost nothing about real-world performance. The three checks that matter most for marketplace skincare: (1) is the active ingredient percentage actually stated, not just implied; (2) does a sunscreen declare both SPF and PA; (3) is the listing sold by the brand or an authorised seller, not an unverified third party.

India's sun-exposure context and why PA rating specifically matters

Most of India sits in a high-UV-index band year-round, with UVA exposure (the wavelength PA ratings measure) contributing disproportionately to pigmentation and melasma — both common presenting concerns in Indian dermatology. A sunscreen optimised for a temperate-climate market may under-deliver on PA rating even at a high SPF. Checking for an explicit PA rating, not just SPF number, is a genuinely India-relevant filter, not a generic global best practice repeated out of context.

How to read a listing

Sunscreen check
SPF + PA, both
Look for both numbers stated — SPF 50 PA++++ is complete; "SPF 50" alone with no PA anywhere is an incomplete listing, not necessarily a bad product.
Active-ingredient check
% on the label
Niacinamide up to 10%, BHA typically capped at 2% for leave-on, retinol start low (≤0.3%) if new to it. Match % to your tolerance, not the highest number available.
Seller check
"Sold by" field
Amazon.in or a named, brand-authorised seller reduces counterfeit risk meaningfully versus an unbranded third-party seller, especially for imported or prestige skincare.
Compliance check
CDSCO, not FSSAI
Cosmetics sit under CDSCO/Drugs & Cosmetics Act, not FSSAI. CDSCO confirms manufacturing/import compliance — it does not certify a "brightening" or "anti-ageing" claim.

Frequently asked

What does SPF 50 PA++++ actually mean?
SPF measures UVB protection (the rays that cause sunburn); PA measures UVA protection (the rays linked to pigmentation and premature ageing) on a scale of PA+ to PA++++. SPF 50 PA++++ means high protection against both. A product listing only SPF with no PA rating is giving you half the picture — common on marketplace listings that copy only the SPF number from the box.
Is a higher percentage of an active ingredient always better?
No. Niacinamide is well-tolerated up to 10%; salicylic acid (BHA) is typically capped at 2% for leave-on use; retinol above 0.3% for beginners often causes irritation before benefit. Higher percentage increases irritation risk faster than it increases efficacy past a compound-specific threshold. Match the percentage to your skin's tolerance, not the highest number on the shelf.
Does CDSCO approval mean a skincare product is clinically tested?
No. CDSCO registration confirms manufacturing licence and import compliance under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act — it does not require clinical efficacy testing for cosmetic claims like "brightening" or "anti-ageing." Treat CDSCO compliance as a manufacturing-safety baseline, not proof a claim works.
Is imported skincare on Amazon.in safe to buy?
Generally yes if sold by Amazon or a named authorised seller. Grey-market imports sold by unverified third parties carry higher counterfeit and formulation-tampering risk, particularly for higher-value serums and fragrance-adjacent products. Checking the "Sold by" field is the single highest-value habit for this category.
