Sulphate-free ≠ better
"Sulphate-free" is a formulation choice, not a quality signal. Sulphate-free surfactant systems (typically milder, lower-lather) suit coloured or chemically treated hair; standard sulphate systems clean more effectively for oily scalps. Neither is objectively "better" — the right pick depends on hair type.

Common questions

Frequently asked

Is Luxury Beauty on Amazon India actually authentic?
Only if the listing is sold or fulfilled by Amazon, or by a brand-authorised seller shown on the product page under "Sold by." Third-party sellers with no brand authorisation are the highest-risk source for counterfeit fragrance and skincare on Indian marketplaces. Check the seller name before adding to cart, not after.
Why does the same SPF number protect differently?
SPF measures UVB protection only. PA+ ratings (PA+ through PA++++) measure UVA protection separately, under the JCIA standard most Indian sunscreen brands now follow voluntarily. A high SPF with no PA rating, or only PA+, leaves UVA-driven pigmentation and premature ageing largely unaddressed — relevant given India's year-round UV index.
EDT vs EDP — does it matter which one I buy?
Yes. Eau de Parfum (EDP) has a higher concentration of aromatic compounds (typically 15–20%) than Eau de Toilette (EDT, 5–15%), giving longer wear and deeper projection. Listings that show only "Perfume" or "Fragrance" without EDT/EDP/Cologne stated on the label or bottle image are the most common source of buyer disappointment on marketplace fragrance.
Does FSSAI or BIS regulate cosmetics in India?
Neither. Cosmetics fall under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act via the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO), which requires manufacturing licences and import registration — not batch potency testing. A CDSCO-compliant label is a manufacturing/import compliance signal, not a clinical efficacy guarantee, the same logic we apply to FSSAI on supplements.
Why do some prices on this page say "placeholder"?
This page is a layout prototype. The scoring format, filter bar, and card system are production-ready and match the rest of Naked Compound's Best Of pages — but the specific products, prices, and stats shown are illustrative until real ASINs and verified pricing are swapped in.
How is this different from Amazon's own category pages?
Amazon's category and bestseller pages are algorithmic and unfiltered — anyone can list, and rank can be bought with ad spend or deal pricing. This page applies the same editorial filter Naked Compound uses for supplements: seller/authenticity checks, label-claim literacy, and a documented "why we picked this" for every product, with no brand paying for placement.
