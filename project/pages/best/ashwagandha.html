Scoring rubric

How we score →

1. Evidence quality

8.5/10

24+ RCTs specifically on KSM-66. Pratte et al. (2014) meta-analysis across 5 RCTs confirms 14–32% cortisol reduction vs placebo. Chandrasekhar et al. (2012) is the landmark cortisol + PSS stress RCT. Wankhede et al. (2015) confirmed muscle performance benefits. Sensoril has 14+ additional RCTs. Deduction of 1.5 because evidence applies only to licensed extracts, not to the generic products dominating the Indian market.

2. Dosage confidence

8.0/10

KSM-66: 300–600mg/day. 600mg most-studied. Sensoril: 125–250mg/day. Dose-response is clear within trial ranges. Minor uncertainty: optimal dose may vary by application (stress vs muscle vs sleep), and no head-to-head dose-finding studies exist for the Indian population specifically.

3. India market fit

8.5/10

India produces over 90% of the world's ashwagandha root. FSSAI Schedule II compliant. Licensed KSM-66 products are available at ₹9.98–₹13.3/day — genuinely accessible. Stress, sleep quality, and training performance are the primary Indian urban use cases. The Ayurvedic tradition gives the ingredient cultural acceptance that accelerates uptake.

4. Safety profile

8.0/10

KSM-66 at 600mg/day tested up to 16 weeks — no significant adverse events. Rare hepatotoxicity case reports exist (mostly unlicensed extracts at >1g/day). Thyroid modulation (T3/T4 elevation) is documented — relevant for individuals on thyroid medications. Avoid in pregnancy (uterotonic activity in animal models). Score of 8.0 reflects the legitimate safety signals rather than a clean record.

5. Label accuracy

7.5/10

For licensed KSM-66/Sensoril products: label accuracy is high — the extract licence itself provides the quality guarantee. For the 73% of Indian products using generic extract: label accuracy is unverifiable. A product declaring "5% withanolides" on a generic extract has no independent verification mechanism. The category average is weighted down by market composition.

How to use it

Stress + Training (KSM-66)
600mg/day
The most-studied dose. Take with food. Morning or evening — both work. Consistent daily intake for 8+ weeks to assess full cortisol-modulating effect. Studies show benefit from 4–8 weeks of consistent use.
Sleep + Anxiety (KSM-66)
300mg/day
Deshpande et al. (2020) showed significant sleep improvement at 300mg/day in a crossover RCT. Lower dose option for users primarily targeting sleep quality. Take 1–2 hours before bed.
Cognitive focus (Sensoril)
250mg/day
Sensoril's 10%+ withanolide glycoside content makes it more potent per mg for cognitive endpoints. 125mg twice daily or 250mg once daily. Cognitive effects typically noticeable from week 4.
Cycle length
8–12 weeks
Most RCTs are 8–16 weeks. No evidence of tolerance development. Cycling (8 weeks on, 4 weeks off) is precautionary rather than evidence-based. Continuous use appears safe at standard doses.

Frequently asked

KSM-66 or Sensoril — which should I choose?
KSM-66 for: stress management, cortisol reduction, muscle performance and recovery, general energy. 600mg/day, root-only extract. Sensoril for: cognitive performance, anxiety reduction, sleep quality. 250mg/day, root+leaf extract with higher withanolide density. Both are evidence-backed with licensed extracts. If you're training and stressed, choose KSM-66. If you're managing cognitive workload or anxiety, choose Sensoril. Both can be used simultaneously at their respective doses — no known adverse interaction.
Why is generic ashwagandha inadequate?
24+ RCTs for KSM-66 were conducted on that specific extract from that specific manufacturer using their specific extraction method. Withanolide content, phytochemical profile, and bioavailability all depend on the extraction process. A product declaring "Withania somnifera extract, 5% withanolides" from an unlicensed manufacturer cannot be assumed to have the same phytochemical composition as KSM-66. The evidence does not transfer.
Can ashwagandha affect thyroid hormones?
Yes — documented in multiple RCTs. KSM-66 and other ashwagandha extracts significantly increase T3 and T4 levels (Panda & Kar, 1998; Sharma et al., 2018). This is beneficial for subclinical hypothyroidism but can be problematic for individuals already on thyroid medication (levothyroxine, propylthiouracil). If you take thyroid medication, inform your doctor before starting ashwagandha supplementation.
Is ashwagandha safe during pregnancy?
No. Ashwagandha exhibits uterotonic activity in animal models — it stimulates uterine contractions. It is traditionally contraindicated during pregnancy in Ayurvedic practice, and modern clinical guidance aligns. Avoid ashwagandha supplements during pregnancy and breastfeeding. This contraindication applies to all extract types including KSM-66 and Sensoril.
