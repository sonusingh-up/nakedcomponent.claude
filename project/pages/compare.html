Editorial red flags

What our scores automatically penalise

🚩

ALA-only "omega-3"

Flaxseed and chia products sold as omega-3 supplements. ALA converts to EPA at 5–10% efficiency in most adults, to DHA at <1%. Not equivalent to fish oil for any cardiovascular or cognitive endpoint. Every such product in our database is flagged and penalised on label honesty.

🚩

Generic vs. patented extracts

KSM-66 and Sensoril have extract-specific RCT data. Generic ashwagandha powder, regardless of claimed withanolide %, has no such trial base. Same logic applies: Creapure vs. generic creatine, BCM-95 vs. plain curcumin. The trial data attaches to the extract, not the plant.

🚩

Cyanocobalamin B12

The active, neurologically-relevant form of B12 is methylcobalamin. Cyanocobalamin requires hepatic conversion — a step that fails in individuals with MTHFR variants (~15% of Indians). For vegetarians with borderline deficiency (NFHS-5: 47% of urban Indians), the correct form matters.

🚩

D2 instead of D3

Ergocalciferol (D2) raises serum 25(OH)D approximately half as effectively as cholecalciferol (D3) with a shorter half-life. ICMR and NICE both recommend D3. Indian products using D2 at any dose are rated down on ingredient form — no exceptions.

🚩

Proprietary blends

When multiple actives are hidden behind a single combined weight, underdosing is undetectable and we cannot score dose accuracy. Pre-workouts and testosterone boosters are the worst offenders. No product with a meaningful prop blend scores above 6.5 on dose accuracy.

🚩

"1000mg fish oil" ≠ 1000mg omega-3

A standard 1000mg fish oil capsule contains approximately 180mg EPA + 120mg DHA = 300mg combined omega-3. The cardiovascular benefit threshold is 1g EPA+DHA daily — requiring 3–4 standard capsules. Products that don't disclose EPA+DHA separately are scored down on label honesty.

Scoring methodology

Five questions. Public weights. No product scores above 9 without third-party purity data.

01
Dose accuracy
Is the clinical dose present? Does it match the best RCTs?
02
Ingredient form
Is the bioavailable form used? D3 vs D2, methylcobalamin vs cyano, glycinate vs oxide.
03
Purity documentation
Is a NABL/IFOS/NSF COA published? Is it batch-level?
04
India-market value
₹ per clinically effective dose. Not sticker price — dose-adjusted value.
05
Label honesty
Do claims match evidence? Prop blends, ALA fraud, disease claims — all penalised.
Composite score = weighted average of all five dimensions. Evidence quality modifies the weight — a product in a weak-evidence category (e.g. herbal tonics) can't escape a low evidence-quality penalty by being well-dosed. Full methodology →
