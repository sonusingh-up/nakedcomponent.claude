Editorial red flags

What our scores automatically penalise

🚩

ALA-only "omega-3"

Flaxseed and chia products sold as omega-3. ALA converts to EPA at 5–10% efficiency, DHA at <1%. Not equivalent to fish oil for any cardiovascular or cognitive endpoint.

🚩

Generic vs. patented extracts

KSM-66 and Sensoril have extract-specific RCT data. Generic ashwagandha, regardless of claimed withanolide %, has no such trial base. The evidence attaches to the extract, not the plant.

🚩

Cyanocobalamin B12

Cyanocobalamin requires hepatic conversion. Methylcobalamin is the active neurological form. For India's ~47% B12-deficient urban adults (NFHS-5), the form matters — especially for vegetarians.

🚩

D2 instead of D3

Ergocalciferol raises serum 25(OH)D roughly half as effectively as cholecalciferol. ICMR and NICE both recommend D3. Indian products using D2 are rated down on ingredient form, no exceptions.

🚩

Proprietary blends

When multiple actives hide behind one combined weight, underdosing is undetectable and we cannot score dose accuracy. Pre-workouts and testosterone boosters are the worst offenders.

🚩

"1000mg fish oil" ≠ 1000mg omega-3

A standard 1000mg capsule has ~180mg EPA + 120mg DHA = 300mg combined omega-3. The cardiovascular threshold is 1g EPA+DHA — requiring 3–4 such capsules. Undisclosed EPA/DHA is penalised on label honesty.

Scoring methodology

Five questions. Public weights. No product above 9 without third-party purity data.

01
Dose accuracy
Is the clinical dose present? Does it match the best RCTs for this specific extract?
02
Ingredient form
D3 vs D2, methylcobalamin vs cyano, glycinate vs oxide — bioavailable form selected?
03
Purity documentation
Is a NABL/IFOS/NSF COA published? Is it batch-level or just brand-level?
04
India-market value
₹ per clinically effective dose. Dose-adjusted, not sticker price comparison.
05
Label honesty
Do claims match evidence? Prop blends, ALA fraud, disease claims all penalised.
Composite score = weighted average of all five. Evidence quality modifies weights — a product in a weak-evidence category can't escape a low evidence penalty by being well-dosed. Full methodology →
