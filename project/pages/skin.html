Skin Care · The Edit

The SPF number is only half the story.

PA ratings, active-ingredient percentages, and CDSCO compliance — the label-literacy questions most skin care listings quietly skip, and three picks that don't.

By the Naked Compound Research Team · 6 min read · Updated September 2026

Cosmetics in India sit under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, policed by CDSCO — a manufacturing and import compliance framework, not a clinical-efficacy gatekeeper the way FSSAI functions for supplements. That gap means a "10% vitamin C" or "clinically proven" claim on a skin care listing can be technically legal while telling you almost nothing about how the product actually performs.

We read every listing the way we read a supplement label: for what's stated, what's implied, and what's quietly missing. Three checks matter most — whether a sunscreen declares both SPF and PA, whether an active ingredient's percentage is actually printed, and who the product is sold by. This edit walks through all three, with three real picks that pass.

Do It Yourself

How to read any label.

The same four checks we ran on the picks above — so you can run them on anything else you're considering.

Sunscreen check

SPF + PA, both

Look for both numbers stated — SPF 50 PA++++ is complete; "SPF 50" alone with no PA rating anywhere is an incomplete listing, not necessarily a bad product.

Active-ingredient check

The % on the label

Niacinamide up to ~10%, BHA typically capped near 2% for leave-on, retinol starting low (≤0.3%) if you're new to it. Match the % to your tolerance, not the highest number available.

Seller check

Who it's sold by

Sold directly by the brand or a named, brand-authorised seller reduces counterfeit risk meaningfully versus an unbranded third party — especially for imported or prestige skincare.

Compliance check

CDSCO, not FSSAI

Cosmetics sit under CDSCO and the Drugs & Cosmetics Act, not FSSAI. CDSCO confirms manufacturing/import compliance — it does not certify a "brightening" or "anti-ageing" claim.

Frequently asked
What does SPF 50 PA++++ actually mean?
SPF measures UVB protection (sunburn risk); PA measures UVA protection (pigmentation, photoageing) on a scale from PA+ to PA++++. Indian dermatology guidance generally recommends SPF 50 with at least PA+++ year-round, since UVA-driven pigmentation and melasma are a near-constant risk across most of India.
Is a higher percentage of an active ingredient always better?
No. Niacinamide is well-tolerated up to about 10%; salicylic acid (BHA) is typically capped near 2% for leave-on use; retinol above 0.3% often causes irritation before added benefit for beginners. Past a compound-specific threshold, higher percentage raises irritation risk faster than it raises efficacy — match the number to your skin's tolerance, not the highest one on the shelf.
Does CDSCO approval mean a skincare product is clinically tested?
No. CDSCO registration confirms manufacturing licence and import compliance under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act — it doesn't require clinical efficacy testing for cosmetic claims like "brightening" or "anti-ageing." Treat CDSCO compliance as a manufacturing-safety baseline, not proof a specific claim works.
Is imported skincare bought online safe?
Generally yes when sold directly by the brand or a named, brand-authorised seller. Grey-market imports sold by unverified third parties carry higher counterfeit and tampering risk, particularly for higher-value serums. Checking who the seller is remains the single highest-value habit in this category.
