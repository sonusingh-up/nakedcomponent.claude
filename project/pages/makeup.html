Shade range is the whole review.

Undertone coverage over hero-image swatches, oxidation behaviour over marketing copy, and three picks that hold up past the first application.

By the Naked Compound Research Team· 5 min read· Updated September 2026

Shade-match failure is the single largest driver of makeup returns bought online — not fabric quality, not price, not delivery time. A hero image showing six shades on six visibly similar skin tones tells you almost nothing about whether a brand actually covers the range of undertones common across India.

We read makeup and nail listings for what the shade chart actually shows, whether oxidation or wear-time claims are backed by real reviews rather than marketing copy, and whether "for all skin types" is doing any real work. Three picks below, and the checks to run on anything else.

Do It Yourself

How to check any listing.

Shade check

The full chart

Look past the hero image to the complete shade chart — count the shades genuinely covering medium-to-deep undertones, not just the total number listed.

Oxidation check

Search reviews for the word

Reviews specifically mentioning "oxidises" or "goes darker" are more reliable than the product description for predicting real wear.

Wear-claim check

Directional, not literal

"24-hour wear" and "transfer-proof" aren't independently certified. Weight verified-purchase reviews over the claim itself.

Patch-test reminder

Especially for new brands

A small patch test 24–48 hours before full-face application catches most reactions before they're visible on your face.

Frequently asked
Does shade range actually matter that much?
Yes — shade mismatch is the single largest driver of makeup returns bought online. A brand showing 6-8 shades in a hero image often carries more shades that aren't pictured; check the full shade chart on the listing, not just the cover photo, before choosing by eye.
Why does foundation oxidise and go darker after application?
Oxidation happens when a foundation's pigments react with air and skin oil over a few hours, shifting the shade darker or warmer than it appeared at swatch. Formulas with heavier oil content oxidise more; if a listing doesn't mention oxidation behaviour at all, it's worth checking reviews specifically for that word.
What does "24-hour wear" or "transfer-proof" actually guarantee?
These are marketing claims, not standardised test results — there's no regulatory body verifying wear-time claims on cosmetics. Treat them as directional rather than literal, and weight verified reviews mentioning real-world wear time more heavily than the claim itself.
Is nail lacquer regulated for chip-resistance claims?
No. "Chip-resistant" or "7-day wear" on nail lacquer is a brand claim, not a certified standard. Base coat use, top coat reapplication, and individual nail chemistry affect real-world wear far more than the product alone.
