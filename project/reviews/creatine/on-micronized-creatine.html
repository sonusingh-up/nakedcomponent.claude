Creatine monohydrate — why the form matters

Creatine monohydrate is the reference form in the supplement literature. Of the 1,000+ published creatine trials, effectively all use monohydrate — specifically because it is the most bioavailable form with the lowest molecular weight, delivering the most creatine per gram of supplement consumed.1 Alternative forms (HCl, buffered, ethyl ester, kre-alkalyn) have consistently failed to demonstrate superiority over monohydrate at matched doses in head-to-head RCTs despite higher prices.3

The "micronized" designation matters for one practical reason: particle size. Micronized creatine is milled to a finer particle size than standard monohydrate, improving dissolution in water and reducing the gritty residue that non-micronized creatine can leave. It does not change the molecule or the mechanism — creatine is creatine — but it makes the product easier to use in any liquid. ON uses a pharmaceutical-grade micronization process consistent with their US manufacturing standards.

ℹ
What "micronized" actually means for creatine

Micronization reduces particle size to below 200 microns. For creatine monohydrate, this improves dissolution rate and uniformity in water — particularly cold water — and reduces clumping in humid conditions. The biochemical effect is identical to non-micronized creatine; the difference is purely ergonomic. For Indian consumers storing supplements in humid climates, micronized creatine is the more practical form.

The Labdoor A+ rating

Labdoor is a US-based independent supplement testing organisation that grades products on content accuracy, nutritional value, and contamination risk. ON Micronized Creatine has maintained an A+ rating — their highest tier — across multiple testing cycles. An A+ rating requires creatine content within ±10% of label claim, creatinine (degradation product) below 2%, and absence of concerning contaminants at standard detection limits.

This is not the same as a Creapure certification. Labdoor tests the finished product purchased off the shelf — which is the test that matters for the consumer. AlzChem's Creapure certification covers raw material purity at the point of synthesis. A product can claim Creapure and still suffer degradation in storage or logistics. Labdoor's finished-product testing catches that. The combination of Creapure sourcing plus Labdoor testing (as ON achieves) is the gold standard — but Labdoor testing alone at A+ is nearly as strong.

Dosing — the 5g question

ON Micronized Creatine provides 5g per scoop — the correct maintenance dose. The international consensus from both the ISSN position stand and the Buford et al. 2007 meta-analysis is 3–5g/day for creatine maintenance after an optional loading phase.1 Five grams is also the correct loading dose quantum: 4 × 5g scoops = 20g loading dose per day, which saturates intramuscular PCr stores in 5–7 days.2

In an Indian market where 3g scoops are common (and misleadingly cheaper per-tub), the full 5g serving is not guaranteed. ON's 5g scoop has been consistent across product generations and across multiple Labdoor tested batches. The dose is correct. The scoop is accurate.
