The bottom line

Naked Compound verdict · 7.3 / 10

Nutrabay Gold Creatine is essentially the same product as Nutrabay Pure at a ₹50 premium. The documentation approach is identical. Choose Pure unless Gold is on offer.

Nutrabay operates two house brand tiers for creatine: Pure Series (₹649/300g) and Gold Series (₹699/300g). Both publish quarterly batch COAs on the Nutrabay website. Both contain creatine monohydrate at 5g per scoop with no fillers. The primary difference is packaging — Gold Series has premium-finish packaging — and price.

The documentation approach is the same: quarterly COA from an unnamed contract laboratory, not a NABL-accredited facility. The same limitations apply: the COA exists and is publicly accessible, but cannot be independently verified through a national accreditation directory. This gives both products the same purity floor and the same ceiling.

At ₹11.7/dose, Gold costs ₹0.9 more per dose than Pure at ₹10.8. Over 60 doses (one container), that is ₹54 more for the same COA documentation and the same active ingredient. The only justification for Gold over Pure is aesthetics — the Gold packaging is better-presented if that matters for your shelf or gym bag. Otherwise, Pure Series is the rational choice.

COA documentation: the same floor as Pure Series

Nutrabay publishes COAs for both their Pure and Gold creatine products on a quarterly schedule. The COA format covers creatine content (typically 97–101% of label claim), creatinine (the degradation product, typically <2%), heavy metals panel, and moisture. These are the key parameters for creatine monohydrate quality evaluation.

The testing laboratory is unnamed in both COAs. This limits independent verification. You can access the document and read the numbers, but you cannot cross-check the lab against a national accreditation directory to confirm the testing methodology or analyst credentials. This is the common limitation across both Nutrabay house brand products.

⚠
Why naming the lab matters

A NABL-accredited COA includes the testing laboratory's accreditation number, which can be verified at the NABL website (nabl.gov.in). This takes 30 seconds and confirms the lab is real, accredited, and capable of the stated test methods. A COA without a lab name or accreditation number is a document that says what the brand wants it to say — it cannot be independently verified. Nutrabay's documentation is good relative to no documentation; it is not equivalent to NABL-accredited documentation.

Dosing

5g per day, one scoop. Same protocol as all creatine monohydrate products. Store sealed in a cool, dry location. At 300g / 60 doses, one container lasts two months — which spans two quarterly COA cycles. Check the COA published after your purchase date to see if any parameters moved from your batch.
