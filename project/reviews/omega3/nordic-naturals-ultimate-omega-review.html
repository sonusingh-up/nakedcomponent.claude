Mechanism

How EPA and DHA work — and why molecular form changes how much reaches you

EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid, 20:5n-3) and DHA (docosahexaenoic acid, 22:6n-3) are long-chain omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids. Unlike their short-chain precursor ALA, they do not require enzymatic conversion — they are biologically active in the forms taken up from the gut.

Pathway 1 — EPA and the eicosanoid cascade

EPA competes with arachidonic acid (AA, 20:4n-6) for the same cyclooxygenase (COX-1/COX-2) and lipoxygenase (LOX-5/LOX-12) enzymes that generate inflammatory eicosanoids. AA-derived eicosanoids (prostaglandin E2, leukotriene B4, thromboxane A2) are pro-inflammatory and pro-thrombotic. EPA-derived equivalents (prostaglandin E3, leukotriene B5) are two to four orders of magnitude less potent biologically. Increasing dietary EPA shifts the membrane phospholipid ratio, blunting the AA-driven signal without eliminating it. RCT

Additionally, EPA is a precursor for E-series resolvins and protectins — lipid mediators that actively resolve inflammation rather than merely suppressing it. This is a qualitatively different mechanism from NSAIDs, which block eicosanoid synthesis entirely. In vitro / animal

Pathway 2 — DHA and membrane structure

DHA is the most structurally important fatty acid in neuronal cell membranes, comprising approximately 97% of the brain's omega-3 content and 93% of retinal omega-3s. Its six double bonds confer exceptional membrane fluidity — critical for ion channel gating speed, receptor conformational change, and synaptic vesicle fusion. DHA also generates D-series resolvins and neuroprotectin D1, which regulate microglial activation and neuroinflammatory cascades. Observational / mechanistic

Why triglyceride form matters

Most concentrated fish oils on the market — including many Indian brands — are produced and sold as ethyl esters (EE). Ethyl esters are formed when a synthetic ethanol molecule bonds to the fatty acid chain during molecular distillation. This is a processing shortcut: EE form is cheaper to concentrate and purify. The problem is that ethyl esters are a non-physiological fat structure not found in nature. The gut hydrolyses them at approximately 68% the efficiency of triglycerides, requiring pancreatic lipase-driven cleavage of the ethanol bond first.

Bioavailability comparison — TG vs rTG vs EE forms (Dyerberg et al., 2010)

Source: Dyerberg J et al. (2010). Bioavailability of marine n-3 fatty acid formulations. Prostaglandins Leukot Essent Fatty Acids, 83(3):137–141. RCT

What this means practically: if you take the same label dose of an EE-form fish oil vs an rTG-form fish oil, you absorb approximately 70% as much EPA+DHA from the EE version. The 650 mg EPA + 450 mg DHA on Nordic Naturals' label therefore delivers meaningfully more to circulation than the same stated dose from an EE product — roughly equivalent to ~890 mg EPA + 615 mg DHA from a standard EE softgel.

Dose context: AHA, ESC, and ICMR recommendations

The American Heart Association recommends ≥500 mg EPA+DHA/day for general cardiovascular support, and 1,000 mg/day for patients with established coronary artery disease. The European Society of Cardiology echoes this. India's ICMR-NIN 2020 guidelines recommend 250 mg/day DHA for general adults. Nordic Naturals Ultimate Omega at 2 softgels/day delivers 1,100 mg EPA+DHA — meeting all three thresholds in a single serving. At 4 softgels/day (the "ultimate support" suggestion on the label), it reaches the lower boundary of triglyceride-lowering doses studied in clinical trials.

Key concern

Two things to watch: oil freshness in India's supply chain, and the import cost reality

1. Oil freshness — the universal fish oil problem

Nordic Naturals' manufacturing uses an oxygen-free process from extraction through encapsulation — nitrogen blanketing at each transfer step, TOTOX measurement throughout. This is industry-best practice, and it results in genuinely fresh oil at the point of production. The problem for Indian consumers is what happens after the product leaves the Norwegian or US manufacturing facility.

Products listed on Amazon India by third-party importers — which is the typical channel for Nordic Naturals in India — pass through ambient-temperature sea freight (14–28 days), Indian customs warehousing, Amazon fulfilment centres, and last-mile delivery. During Indian summer (March–June), ambient temperatures in logistics hubs in Delhi, Mumbai, and Hyderabad regularly exceed 42°C. Fish oil oxidises exponentially above 25°C. There is no cold-chain standard enforced for supplement imports in India under current FSSAI guidelines.

How to verify freshness on receipt

Cut open one softgel when the bottle arrives. Nordic Naturals' fresh oil has a clean, mild lemon scent. If you smell rancid fish, stale cooking oil, or sharp pungency — the oil has oxidised in transit. Oxidised fish oil is not merely ineffective; aldehyde byproducts of lipid peroxidation are biologically active and potentially pro-inflammatory. Do not consume a rancid batch — request a replacement or refund. Check the expiry date: avoid stock with fewer than 12 months remaining, as TOTOX rises with age even at correct storage temperature.

Evidence

What the clinical record shows for the dose this product delivers

Cardiovascular endpoints

The JELIS trial (Yokoyama et al., 2007; n=18,645) found a 19% reduction in major coronary events with 1,800 mg/day pure EPA added to statin therapy in Japanese hypercholesterolaemic patients. RCT The REDUCE-IT trial (Bhatt et al., 2019; n=8,179) found a 25% relative risk reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events with 4,000 mg/day icosapentaenoic acid (prescription-strength EPA). RCT Note: the REDUCE-IT mineral oil placebo controversy — some researchers argue the placebo (mineral oil) may have raised LDL in the control group, inflating the apparent benefit. This remains an active methodological debate in cardiology.

At Nordic Naturals Ultimate Omega's standard dose (2 softgels = 1,100 mg EPA+DHA), the product falls within the maintenance/prevention range endorsed by the AHA for general population cardiovascular support, but below the therapeutic doses used in the major outcome trials. This is not a criticism of the product — it is a calibration note. For triglyceride management, the evidence-based dose is 3,000–4,000 mg/day EPA+DHA; you would need 5–6 softgels of this product daily. Meta-analysis

Brain and cognitive function

The MIDAS trial (Yurko-Mauro et al., 2010; n=485) found statistically significant improvements in episodic memory in healthy older adults supplemented with 900 mg/day DHA for 24 weeks vs placebo. RCT Nordic Naturals Ultimate Omega provides 450 mg DHA/serving — at 2 servings/day, it would match the MIDAS dose. At the standard single serving, it delivers half. Most mood-related RCTs (EPA for MDD, anxiety) use EPA doses of 1,000–2,000 mg/day; this product's single-serving EPA of 650 mg is at the lower boundary of demonstrated effect for mood endpoints. Meta-analysis

Eye health and retinal function

DHA is essential for photoreceptor membrane structure and rhodopsin function. The AREDS2 trial added EPA+DHA to the original AREDS antioxidant formula — the combined supplement did not significantly further reduce AMD progression over the antioxidant formula alone. RCT However, observational data consistently links higher habitual omega-3 intake with lower risk of AMD onset. Observational For dry eye specifically, 3–4 softgels/day of this product (or a higher-potency EPA formulation) has more consistent RCT support than a single daily serving. RCT

Joint and inflammatory markers

A 2018 Cochrane review (Goldberg & Katz) of omega-3 supplementation in rheumatoid arthritis found statistically significant reductions in joint pain intensity and morning stiffness with EPA+DHA doses of 1,800–2,700 mg/day. Meta-analysis The standard 2-softgel serving here provides 1,100 mg — meaningful, but dose-response relationships in joint inflammation RCTs suggest better results at higher doses.

India market context

Who actually needs supplemental EPA+DHA in India — and at what dose

Import Nordic Naturals Ultimate Omega is an imported product — manufactured in the US, typically imported under a registered Indian importer's FSSAI licence. The importer's FSSAI licence covers import compliance, not independent quality verification of the product inside the bottle. Always check the importer name and FSSAI number on the Indian label/sticker before purchasing — this confirms you are buying a legitimately imported unit, not a grey-market or counterfeit product.

The Indian dietary context for omega-3

India has a complex omega-3 landscape. Coastal populations — Tamil Nadu, Kerala, West Bengal, Goa, Odisha, coastal Andhra Pradesh — consume hilsa, mackerel, sardine, and anchovies regularly. In these populations, supplemental EPA+DHA is often unnecessary unless prescribed for a specific indication. A single serving of fresh hilsa (rohu ilish) provides approximately 1,200–1,500 mg EPA+DHA — more than this supplement's daily dose.

The populations most likely to genuinely benefit from this supplement: strict vegetarians and vegans (for whom algae oil remains the only truly appropriate choice), inland urban professionals cooking in refined seed oils with minimal fish intake, pregnant women who avoid or minimise fish (DHA is critical for foetal neurodevelopment — 200 mg DHA/day is the European EFSA recommendation for pregnancy), patients with diagnosed hypertriglyceridaemia (in whom 3–4g/day EPA+DHA is used clinically), and individuals with elevated inflammatory markers or post-cardiac event risk who have been directed to omega-3 supplementation by a physician.

Note on vegetarians and vegans

Nordic Naturals Ultimate Omega is derived from fish and contains bovine gelatin in the softgel shell. It is unsuitable for vegetarians and vegans. The correct alternative for this population is algae-derived EPA+DHA — see our omega-3 ingredient entry for a full comparison of algae oil options available in India.

What the brand should fix

Three things Nordic Naturals should do for the Indian market specifically

1

Publish batch COAs online, indexed by lot number

Currently, obtaining a COA requires phoning or emailing Nordic Naturals directly with a lot number. This is unrealistic for the vast majority of Indian consumers buying through Amazon. Competitor Now Foods posts batch-level IFOS test results publicly at ifos.com — searchable by lot number, freely accessible. Nordic Naturals should either enrol in IFOS or build a public COA portal. The data clearly supports the product. Hiding it behind a phone call serves no one except consumers who never check.

2

Establish an authorised Indian cold-chain distribution agreement

Nordic Naturals' own quality standards require oxygen-free manufacturing and controlled storage. The brand has no publicly known cold-chain distribution agreement in India. Most Indian stock moves through ambient-temperature freight and unrefrigerated Amazon fulfilment. The brand should appoint an authorised importer with documented cold-chain handling from port to consumer — and make that information visible on their India product pages. Pharmavite (owner of Nature Made) and several ayurvedic brands have managed this for temperature-sensitive products.

3

Launch a fish-gelatin or halal-certified variant for the Indian market

Nordic Naturals already produces a fish-gelatin softgel version for pescatarians. Muslim consumers in India — a significant supplement-buying demographic — cannot consume bovine gelatin without halal certification. The current India-market stock uses standard bovine gelatin with no halal certification. Making the fish-gelatin variant the default for India distribution, or pursuing halal certification for the existing formulation, would open a large underserved market segment that currently defaults to domestic brands by necessity.
