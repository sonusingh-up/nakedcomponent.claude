How D3 and K2 actually work together

This isn't a marketing bundle. There is a real, well-characterised biochemical reason these two vitamins are paired.

The D3 pathway

Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) is converted first to 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] in the liver, then to the active hormone 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol) in the kidney. Calcitriol binds the vitamin D receptor (VDR), a nuclear receptor expressed in intestinal cells, osteoblasts, immune cells, and parathyroid tissue. The VDR–calcitriol complex upregulates calbindin-D9k and calbindin-D28k, increasing active calcium absorption from the duodenum by 30–40%. (observational + RCT)

Where K2 comes in: the carboxylation requirement

Calcitriol also activates two critical calcium-directing proteins: Matrix Gla Protein (MGP) and osteocalcin. Both require post-translational γ-carboxylation to become biologically active. This carboxylation step is entirely dependent on Vitamin K2 as cofactor — specifically, K2 serves as the electron donor for the γ-glutamyl carboxylase enzyme. (in vitro + observational)

Without adequate K2:

  • Undercarboxylated MGP (ucMGP) accumulates. MGP is the most potent inhibitor of vascular calcification known — but only in its carboxylated form. ucMGP cannot bind calcium crystals in arterial walls, leading to soft-tissue and arterial calcification. (observational)
  • Undercarboxylated osteocalcin cannot bind hydroxyapatite in bone matrix. Bone mineralisation is impaired even when D3 levels are adequate. (observational)
ℹ
The critical point for Indian supplementers

India's most commonly prescribed D3 product — the 60,000 IU weekly sachet (Calcirol, Uprise-D3, D-Rise) — contains no K2. The protocol that corrects D3 levels in millions of Indians systematically excludes the cofactor needed to direct that calcium correctly. This is the real clinical gap that any D3+K2 product addresses.

The 45,000 mcg context matters

The Japanese MK-4 trials commonly cited in supplement marketing — including by Thorne — used 45,000 mcg/day as a pharmaceutical intervention for osteoporosis. Thorne's serving delivers 200 mcg. The 225× dose gap means those RCTs cannot be used to validate efficacy at this product's dose. MK-4 at 200 mcg has mechanistic support but lacks supplement-range RCT evidence for either bone or cardiovascular endpoints. MK-7 at 180 mcg has direct RCT support at this dose range. (RCT)

This is not an argument that MK-4 is "wrong" — it has legitimate tissue-specific roles in brain, testes, and arterial wall, and it is the endogenously synthesised form of K2 in human tissue. But for the typical Indian supplement buyer whose goal is bone health and arterial calcification prevention, MK-7 at supplement-range doses has stronger and more directly applicable evidence. Thorne's choice of MK-4 is defensible; it is not the form-optimal choice.

Evidence by endpoint

Vitamin D deficiency correction

Strong evidence base across the entire category. ICMR (2020) estimates >70% of Indians are deficient or insufficient in vitamin D. Supplementation with D3 at 1,000–2,000 IU/day raises 25(OH)D by approximately 5–20 ng/mL depending on baseline, body weight, and fat mass — consistent across multiple meta-analyses. (meta-analysis) This is not a product-specific claim; any well-formulated D3 supplement achieves this.

Bone mineral density

D3 supplementation alone shows inconsistent benefit on BMD when baseline 25(OH)D is above 20 ng/mL. The addition of K2 (specifically MK-7 in the Knapen et al. 2013 ECCO2 trial, 3-year duration, postmenopausal women) showed significant preservation of femoral neck and lumbar spine BMD versus placebo. (RCT) Note: Knapen et al. used MK-7 at 180 mcg/day — this product uses MK-4 at 200 mcg/day. Direct extrapolation is not appropriate.

Vascular calcification prevention

The Rotterdam Study (Geleijnse et al., 2004, J Nutr) found dietary K2 intake (MK-7 and MK-8 from fermented foods) inversely associated with coronary heart disease risk and aortic calcification in a population-based cohort (n=4,807). (observational) Gast et al. (2009) replicated the association. Mechanistically, this is the MGP/carboxylation pathway. Supplement-range RCT evidence for vascular endpoints specifically is limited across all K2 forms.

Immune function

D3 has well-characterised immunomodulatory effects via VDR expression in macrophages, dendritic cells, and T-cells. Deficiency is consistently associated with higher susceptibility to respiratory infections in observational data. The VITAL trial (RCT, n=25,871) found no significant reduction in cancer incidence at 2,000 IU/day; respiratory infection benefit is more modest than frequently claimed. (RCT)

FSSAI and Schedule K — the regulatory reality

Under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, all supplements sold commercially in India must be registered or licensed under FSSAI. Thorne D/K2 is not FSSAI registered. When purchased through Amazon India's international marketplace or grey-channel importers, it arrives without an FSSAI license number, Indian importer details, or Schedule K-compliant labelling.

⚠
What non-FSSAI status means in practice

Schedule K of the FSSAI Nutraceuticals Regulations (2022) caps Vitamin D in supplements at 2,000 IU/day for unsupervised adult use. Thorne's label allows up to 6 drops (3,000 IU) — which technically exceeds Schedule K limits for nutraceuticals sold in India. Additionally, batch traceability through FSSAI's recall system is not available. If Thorne issues a batch-level quality notice, Indian consumers who purchased via grey-channel importers have no automatic notification mechanism. Consumer recourse through FSSAI's portal requires a registered Indian importer — absent here.

Dietary context: who in India actually needs D3+K2?

Vitamin D deficiency is genuinely ubiquitous in India — it cuts across every demographic. The highest-risk groups are urban office workers with minimal sun exposure (Bengaluru tech workers, Delhi NCR commuters), vegetarians (no dietary D3 from fish or meat), women who cover skin outdoors, and older adults with reduced skin synthesis efficiency. The Indian vegetarian diet also tends to be low in naturally-occurring K2 — natto, the Japanese fermented soybean food that is the richest dietary source, is essentially absent from Indian diets. Dairy (paneer, ghee) and fermented foods (some curd, idli) contribute minor K2. Most Indians supplementing D3 are genuinely K2-deficient as well.

Storage note — relevant for this liquid product

🌡
Climate storage — critical for Indian buyers

Thorne D/K2 is an oil-based liquid. MCT oil is relatively stable compared to fish oil, but fat-soluble vitamins in solution degrade faster at high temperatures. Store below 25°C; refrigeration is acceptable. Imported bottles sitting in a Mumbai or Chennai last-mile delivery van in May (ambient temperatures 38–44°C) may have experienced heat exposure. Once opened, keep refrigerated and use within the stated shelf life. This is a meaningful concern for grey-channel imports where cold-chain handling is not guaranteed.

Dosing protocol

Per drop: ~500 IU D3 + ~100 mcg MK-4. Per serving (2 drops): 1,000 IU D3 + 200 mcg MK-4.

Maintenance (serum 25-OHD ≥ 40 ng/mL): 2 drops daily with a fat-containing meal. Mild deficiency correction (25–39 ng/mL): 4 drops (2,000 IU) for 8–12 weeks, then retest. Moderate deficiency (below 20 ng/mL): physician-directed correction first; transition to maintenance dosing when serum levels are restored. Do not exceed 4,000 IU/day without clinical oversight. A serum vitamin D test (25-OHD) costs ₹300–800 at any diagnostic lab in India — do this before supplementing above 2,000 IU/day.

Safety and interactions

At 1,000–2,000 IU/day, vitamin D toxicity (hypercalcaemia) is not a realistic concern. Sustained dosing above 4,000 IU/day without testing warrants attention. K2 interacts with warfarin and other vitamin K-dependent anticoagulants — consult your physician before supplementing any K2 product if on anticoagulant therapy. Magnesium deficiency (very common in urban Indians on processed diets) can limit D3 activation — if supplementing D3 without improvement, investigate serum magnesium. Corticosteroids, rifampicin, antacids, and anti-epileptics all deplete D3; higher replacement doses may be needed under clinical supervision.

What Thorne should fix

1

Switch to MK-7 or offer an MK-7 variant for the Indian market

MK-7 at 180–200 mcg per serving has direct RCT evidence at the actual supplement dose. Thorne's choice of MK-4 at 200 mcg creates a 225× dose gap with the trials that support it. An MK-7 formulation at the same price point would be scientifically superior for the bone and cardiovascular use cases that drive D3+K2 purchases.

2

Actively correct per-drop dosing misinformation in the Indian market channel

Multiple Amazon India listings and third-party review sites state "1,000 IU per drop." This is incorrect — the serving is 2 drops. Thorne does not appear to have actively corrected this in the Indian channel. Incorrect dosing information for a fat-soluble vitamin with a toxicity threshold is a label honesty issue by omission.

3

Register with FSSAI through an authorised Indian distributor

Thorne's current Indian market presence is entirely through grey-channel imports. An FSSAI-licensed distributor relationship would provide batch traceability, Schedule K compliance, and consumer recourse — and would differentiate Thorne from smaller grey-market imports. The NSF certification Thorne already holds would make FSSAI approval a straightforward process.
