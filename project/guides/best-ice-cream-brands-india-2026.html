The answer before the detail
India's ice cream market has split into three tiers. Pick your tier first, then pick your brand — not the other way around.

Every honest guide to ice cream in India in 2026 has to start here: Amul and Noto are not competing for the same customer. Naturals and Kwality Wall's Magnum are not in the same conversation. Treating them as interchangeable options in one ranked list — which most guides do — is why people end up disappointed. Once you know which of the three tiers matches your actual need, the best brand inside that tier becomes obvious. That's what this guide does.

The three tiers — your map before the list

Between 2021 and 2026, something structurally interesting happened to the Indian ice cream market. It stopped being a single market. A new wave of high-protein, no-added-sugar brands (Noto, Go Zero) emerged and found real distribution through Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart and Zepto. A revitalized artisanal tier — led by Naturals and NIC — grew well beyond their home cities. And the scale players (Amul, Vadilal, Kwality Wall's) consolidated and got better at their own lane while leaving the premiumisation play to everyone else.

These three tiers answer completely different questions. Buy from the wrong tier and no amount of "best brand" research will save you from disappointment.

Tier 1 — Health-First Ice Cream
Noto · Go Zero

The emergence of this tier is the single most significant structural change to Indian ice cream since Naturals proved real-fruit ice cream could scale. In four years, Noto and Go Zero went from novelty to genuine quick-commerce staples in metros — fuelled by a generation of Indians who were already reading supplement labels, counting protein, and asking why ice cream couldn't do the same.

The key distinction: these brands do not just reduce sugar cosmetically. They reformulate from scratch, using erythritol, stevia, and in Noto's case a high-protein base, so the ice cream genuinely functions differently in the body — lower glycaemic impact, higher protein per 100ml — not just a "light" version of a regular recipe with slightly less sugar added.

🏆 Best Health-First Pick Sugar-free High protein
Noto
Sugar-free · High-protein ice cream cups & tubs
📍 India · Available: Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart, Zepto, D2C

If Naturals made India believe that three simple ingredients were enough to make great ice cream, Noto made urban India believe that zero added sugar doesn't have to mean zero pleasure. That is a genuinely hard problem to solve — and the reason Noto took off is that it actually solved it, rather than just putting "healthy" on packaging for something that still tasted like compromise.

The formulation uses a blend of erythritol and stevia as the sweetener base, with whey protein integrated into several flavours to push the protein-per-100ml figure meaningfully higher than any other brand in this guide. The texture is creamy and genuinely indulgent — not the slightly icy, slightly thin consistency that plagued early reduced-fat ice cream attempts in India a decade ago. Flavours like salted caramel and dark chocolate have developed real cult followings in fitness-adjacent communities in Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Delhi.

The honest caveats: Noto's pricing (₹180–299 per 100ml cup) is the highest of any brand here on a per-unit basis, and availability is still concentrated in metros with good quick-commerce infrastructure. If you're in a Tier-2 city or dependent on offline retail, Go Zero has broader physical footprint. But for a health-first ice cream that does not ask you to accept a taste penalty, Noto is the current standard.

Go Zero
High-protein · Low-calorie · Sugar-free · Plant-based
📍 Founded 2022 by Kiran Shah · Quick commerce + D2C

Go Zero launched in July 2022 with a sharper positioning than most new-age food brands dare: not just "healthier ice cream" but "the exact taste of regular ice cream, with no sugar and high protein." Founder Kiran Shah's decade in the ice cream industry before founding Go Zero shows — the products genuinely match indulgent ice cream on texture and sweetness in a way that earlier "healthy" iterations in India didn't manage.

What distinguishes Go Zero from Noto is the breadth of its positioning: it covers sugar-free, low-calorie, high-protein, and plant-based lines under one brand umbrella, whereas Noto is more focused on the high-protein, sugar-free angle. For someone who wants a dairy-free ice cream option that doesn't sacrifice the health proposition either, Go Zero currently has the strongest India-market offering.

The brand has grown fast on Swiggy and Zomato in particular — quick commerce is Go Zero's primary acquisition channel, which is smart given that ice cream's cold chain requirements make the category a natural fit for 10-minute delivery rather than traditional retail.

Tier 2 — Artisanal & Flavor-First
Naturals Ice Cream · NIC Ice Creams

These brands exist to answer a completely different question from Tier 1: not "how do I enjoy ice cream with less guilt?" but "what does ice cream actually taste like when it's made with real alphonso mango, or real tender coconut, rather than synthetic mango flavour 5724-C?" The answer, if you've ever had a Naturals sitafal scoop, is so obviously different that it raises uncomfortable questions about what you've been eating your whole life.

The artisanal tier doesn't compete on health claims (both Naturals and NIC use real sugar). It competes on ingredient integrity and flavor authenticity — and the loyal, almost evangelical customer bases both brands have built over decades suggest that for a significant slice of Indian consumers, that trade-off is not even a trade-off. It's just the better ice cream.

🏆 Best Artisanal Pick No artificial flavours Since 1984
Naturals Ice Cream
Real-fruit ice cream · 170+ outlets · 15+ states
📍 Founded 1984, Juhu Mumbai · Raghunandan Srinivas Kamath · ₹200+ crore revenue

There are very few food brands in India that have genuinely built their entire business on a formula of three ingredients and kept it there for 42 years. Naturals is one of them: fresh fruit, milk, sugar. That's the whole recipe. No artificial colours, no flavours, no preservatives, no stabilisers — an ingredient list so short it would look at home on the packaging of a brand that launched yesterday positioning itself as a disruptor. The joke is that Naturals was doing this in 1984.

The products that made the brand legendary — Tender Coconut, Sitafal (custard apple), seasonal Alphonso Mango — remain exactly what they were, except you can now find them across 170+ outlets in 15+ states rather than just the original Juhu store in Mumbai. In May 2026, the brand partnered with Bombay Sweet Shop to launch limited-edition Tender Coconut Naga Chilli and Coffee Fudge Crunch flavours, which is the most ambitious product extension they've attempted — a good sign that the brand is confident enough in its foundation to experiment without abandoning it.

The honest limitation of Naturals is that it's still a primarily outlet-based experience rather than an at-home product. You go to a Naturals store; you don't usually find it in a supermarket. That's by design — the three-ingredient formula means the shelf life and cold chain requirements are tighter than a mass-market product with preservatives. It's a feature of the model, not a flaw, but it does mean Naturals is not always the most convenient pick.

NIC Ice Creams
Natural ingredients · Zero preservatives · Premium positioning
📍 Pune-based · ₹171 crore revenue (FY2023) · Expanding nationally

NIC is the closest thing the Indian premium natural ice cream space has to a genuine challenger for Naturals' crown — and unlike many "challenger brands" that announce ambition and then quietly plateau, NIC has actually delivered. Starting from a strong Pune base, the brand has built over 50 flavours on a foundation of pure milk and no preservatives, and expanded its revenue to ₹171 crore as of FY2023 with continued growth since.

Where NIC differentiates from Naturals is in two areas: a larger flavour palette (including seasonal and experimental options that Naturals' minimal formula makes harder to achieve) and better at-home tub availability through quick commerce and select retail channels. If Naturals is the brand you visit when you want the purest possible fruit experience, NIC is the brand you order when you want premium natural ice cream on a Tuesday evening without planning a trip to a specific outlet.

Tier 3 — Everyday Value Brands
Amul · Vadilal · Havmor · Kwality Wall's / Magnum

This is where most ice cream is actually bought in India — not in a quick commerce app at 11pm, not at a specific brand's outlet, but at the neighbourhood kiryana store, at a petrol station, at a supermarket during the weekly shop. The brands in this tier don't compete on "cleanest ingredients" or "most authentic fruit" — they compete on consistent quality at scale, national availability, and pricing that works for daily or weekly consumption rather than the occasional treat.

The mistake would be to read this as "these brands are inferior." They aren't. They're just solving a different problem. The best everyday value brand is not worse than Naturals; it's not the same product at all. It's a consistently made, widely available, appropriately priced ice cream — and in that job, several of these brands are excellent.

🏆 Best Everyday Value India's #1 by volume Nationwide
Amul
India's largest dairy cooperative · Full ice cream range
📍 GCMMF, Anand Gujarat · Widest national availability · ₹20–60 standard range

There is something quietly reassuring about Amul. The cooperative model means the milk comes from a network of dairy farmers rather than a corporate supply chain, the pricing is pegged for genuine national accessibility, and the quality floor has been consistently maintained since the brand's ice cream expansion in the 1990s. Is every Amul flavour an adventure? No. Is every Amul scoop going to be correctly frozen, honestly labelled, and taste like ice cream? Almost always.

In 2026, Amul remains India's market leader by volume — the brand most Indians across income levels and geographies think of when they think "ice cream." That dominance is not coasting; Amul has continued expanding its range (sugar-free variants, kulfi, premium tub sizes) while keeping its entry-level products genuinely affordable at ₹20–40 per serving. The infrastructure for consistent cold chain across 650,000+ villages in India is something no D2C brand or artisanal chain can currently replicate.

Vadilal
Full range · Kulfi · Sugar-free · Exports
📍 Gujarat-based · Founded 1907 · Publicly listed · Strong North & West India presence

Vadilal reported double-digit ice cream volume growth in summer 2026, with plants running at full capacity — notable in a market where several players have complained about input cost pressures. The brand has consistently punched slightly above the "pure commodity" bracket with a genuine flavour range (including an Indian-origin kulfi line and sugar-free variants that have been part of the range for longer than most competitors') while staying meaningfully below premium pricing.

If Amul is the national default and Havmor is the Gujarat-and-North indulgence brand, Vadilal occupies a smart middle position: more variety than Amul's core range, better availability than Havmor outside its home states, and a price point that makes a 750ml family tub an ordinary grocery purchase rather than an occasion spend.

Havmor (Lotte India)
Mid-market · Rich flavour range · North & West India
📍 Founded Gujarat · Acquired Lotte Korea 2017 · Strong North/West presence

Havmor has been making ice cream in Gujarat since the 1940s, and the Lotte acquisition in 2017 gave it the manufacturing and distribution investment to take that heritage national without losing it. The result is a brand that feels genuinely indulgent — richer and creamier than the pure commodity tier — at prices that don't quite reach the premium bracket. In North and West India in particular, it is the everyday celebratory ice cream: what you buy when you want something slightly more special than the default, without paying for Baskin-Robbins.

In April 2026, The Magnum Ice Cream Company completed its majority acquisition of Kwality Wall's India, reporting Q1 organic sales growth of 4.5% — the first major structural change to the brand since Unilever had owned it. The product portfolio — Cornetto, Magnum bars, Feast, the Kwality Wall's Creamy Delight range — remains intact, and the acquisition signals further premiumisation investment in the Indian market.

Kwality Wall's sits at an interesting price position: above Amul and Vadilal on most SKUs, but the Cornetto and Magnum bars justify that premium on experience — the quality of the chocolate coating on a Magnum bar, the wafer crunch on a Cornetto, are genuinely different from a commodity ice cream at two-thirds of the price. For format-specific cravings (cone, bar on a stick, ice cream sandwich), this is where you go.

Frequently asked

Which is the No. 1 ice cream brand in India in 2026?
By revenue and volume, Amul is India's #1 ice cream brand. But that's a market-share answer, not a quality answer. The best brand for you depends on what you're optimising for: Noto for managing sugar intake, Naturals for genuine fruit flavour, and Amul or Vadilal for reliable everyday value. See the verdict grid above.
Is Naturals Ice Cream healthier than regular ice cream?
Naturals uses only three ingredients — fresh fruit, milk, and sugar — with no artificial colours, flavours, or preservatives. That makes it cleaner than most mass-market ice cream on additives. However it still contains full sugar and milk fat, so it isn't low-calorie. For reduced-sugar ice cream, Noto and Go Zero are the better picks. For ingredient transparency without the sugar compromise, Naturals leads.
What is the best sugar-free ice cream brand in India?
Noto and Go Zero are the two leading sugar-free ice cream brands in India as of 2026. Noto is the more established, uses stevia and erythritol, and has several high-protein flavours. Go Zero launched in 2022, expanded fast on quick commerce, and has a broader range including plant-based options. Both are on Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart, and Zepto in major Indian cities.
What happened to Kwality Wall's in 2026?
In April 2026, The Magnum Ice Cream Company completed its majority acquisition of Kwality Wall's India, reporting Q1 organic sales growth of 4.5%. The Magnum, Cornetto, and Kwality Wall's product lines continue operating under the Magnum parent company structure. No product reformulations have been announced.
Is NIC Ice Cream and Naturals Ice Cream the same brand?
No, they are separate brands. Naturals Ice Cream was founded in 1984 by Raghunandan Srinivas Kamath in Mumbai's Juhu neighbourhood and now has 170+ outlets. NIC Ice Creams is a distinct Pune-based brand with 50+ flavours and zero preservatives that has grown rapidly in the premium natural segment. Both use real ingredients and no preservatives, but they are independent businesses.
How big is the Indian ice cream market in 2026?
The Indian ice cream market is estimated at approximately USD 3.07 billion ($25,500 crore) in 2026, projected to reach USD 5.29 billion by 2032 at a CAGR of approximately 9.84%. North India holds the largest regional share at roughly 35%, driven by a large consumer base and strong organized-brand presence in major cities. The better-for-you (health-first) segment is projected to be worth US$500 million by 2030.
