The lineup

Your home pharmacy, decoded.

OTCFever & Pain

Paracetamol 650

Dolo 650 · Crocin Advance · Calpol

Fever, headache, body ache — the safest default first reach for adults.

Typical adult dose*1 tablet every 6–8 hrs · max 4 in 24 hrs (3 g)

CautionCold combos often hide extra paracetamol — never stack brands.

OTCFever & Pain

Ibuprofen 400

Brufen · Ibugesic

Pain with inflammation — sprains, period pain, toothache.

Typical adult dose*400 mg after food, every 6–8 hrs · short courses only

CautionNever on an empty stomach. Avoid in suspected dengue, ulcers, kidney disease.

OTCFever & Pain

Diclofenac gel

Volini · Voveran Emulgel

Localised muscle and joint pain, without swallowing an NSAID.

Typical adult dose*Thin layer on the painful area, 3–4× a day

CautionNot on broken skin. Don't combine with full-dose oral NSAIDs.

OTCStomach

WHO-ORS

Electral · generic WHO-ORS sachets

Rehydration in diarrhoea, vomiting, heat exhaustion. The most life-saving item here.

Typical adult dose*1 sachet in 1 L clean water · sip through the day · discard after 24 hrs

CautionORSL-style tetra packs are fruit drinks, not WHO-ORS. Check the label.

OTCStomach

Antacid gel

Digene · Gelusil

Heartburn, acidity and gas after meals — neutralises acid on contact.

Typical adult dose*2 tsp (or 2 tablets) after meals and at bedtime

CautionAcidity needing antacids beyond 2 weeks needs a doctor, not more gel.

OTCStomach

Fruit salt

ENO

Fast, fizzy relief for occasional heartburn — works in minutes.

Typical adult dose*1 sachet (5 g) in a glass of water, as needed

CautionHigh sodium — go easy with BP, heart or kidney issues. Not a daily habit.

OTCStomach

Isabgol

Psyllium husk · Telephone brand · Dabur

Constipation — a gentle fibre bulk-former, not a stimulant laxative.

Typical adult dose*1–2 tsp in a full glass of water, at night

CautionMust go down with plenty of water. Skip it if you have severe abdominal pain.

OTCStomach

Loperamide 2 mg

Imodium · Eldoper

Stops acute watery diarrhoea when a toilet isn't an option — travel, transit.

Typical adult dose*2 mg after each loose stool · max 3–4 capsules/day · max 2 days

CautionNever with fever or blood in stool, and never for children. ORS comes first.

Typical adult dose*10 mg once daily, preferably at night

Blocked nose, runny nose and cold-day misery, bundled into one tablet.

Vicks VapoRub · Amrutanjan

Blocked-nose comfort, cough-y nights, tension-headache rubs.

Typical adult dose*Rub on chest, back and throat 2–3× a day, or add to steam

OTCSkin & First Aid

Povidone-iodine

Betadine ointment / solution

The first-aid antiseptic for cuts, scrapes and minor burns.

Typical adult dose*Clean the wound, apply a thin layer 1–2× a day, cover

CautionDeep or dirty wounds need a doctor (and possibly a tetanus shot), not just ointment.

Candid · Canesten

Ringworm, athlete's foot and fungal itch — rampant in Indian humidity.

Typical adult dose*2–3× a day for 2–4 weeks · continue 1 week after it clears

CautionAvoid steroid-antifungal combo creams unless prescribed — they make ringworm worse.

Prickly heat, itchy rashes, insect bites, chickenpox itch.

Label literacy

Spot the Schedule H box.

India doesn't print "OTC" on medicines — it marks the ones you shouldn't buy freely. Flip any strip over and look for the warning box and the red vertical line on the pack edge.

Rx
Schedule H — prescription only"To be sold by retail on the prescription of a Registered Medical Practitioner only." Red line on the pack = a pharmacist should ask for a prescription. Antibiotics live here.
✓
No warning box, no red lineGenuinely over-the-counter — like everything featured on this page. Still read the label: dose, expiry, and what's actually inside.

If a chemist hands you a Schedule H drug without a prescription, that's their shortcut — and your risk.

Know when to stop

3 days.

Self-medication has an expiry date too.

  • Fever beyond 3 days, or above 103°F at any point
  • Blood in stool, vomit or urine — in any amount
  • Chest pain, breathlessness, or one-sided weakness
  • Severe or localised abdominal pain
  • A child under 6, pregnancy, or 65+ with a chronic illness
  • The same symptom bouncing back within a week of "getting better"

Any of these means the medicine drawer's job is over. See a doctor — same day.

Asked constantly

The questions every household argues about.

Are Dolo 650, Crocin Advance and Calpol the same medicine?
Yes — all three are paracetamol 650 mg under different brand names. Taking two brands together doubles your paracetamol dose; it does not add a second medicine. Pick one, and never stack brands.
What's the maximum paracetamol an adult can take in a day?
Stay within 3 g per day for self-medication — at most four 650 mg tablets in 24 hours, spaced 6–8 hours apart. The margin shrinks with regular alcohol use, liver disease or low body weight. And count the paracetamol hiding inside cold combos.
Is ORSL the same as WHO-ORS like Electral?
No. ORSL and similar tetra-pack drinks are fruit beverages with a different sugar-salt ratio — they don't meet the WHO oral rehydration formula. For diarrhoea or vomiting, look for "WHO-ORS" printed on the sachet.
Which painkiller is safe during dengue season?
Paracetamol only. Ibuprofen, aspirin and diclofenac raise bleeding risk and are dangerous if the fever turns out to be dengue. Until dengue is ruled out, avoid all NSAIDs.
Can I buy antibiotics over the counter for a cold?
No. Antibiotics are Schedule H — selling them without a prescription is illegal, and colds are viral, so antibiotics do nothing except build resistance. A pharmacist offering azithromycin for a cold is a red flag, not a favour.
Is it okay to use medicine a little past its expiry date?
Don't. Past expiry, potency isn't guaranteed — and Indian heat and humidity (especially bathroom cabinets) degrade medicines faster than the printed date assumes. Audit your home stock twice a year.
