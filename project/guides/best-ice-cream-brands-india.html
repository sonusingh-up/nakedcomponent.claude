The label trick 90% of Indian buyers miss: "Ice Cream" vs "Frozen Dessert"

Under FSSAI regulations, real ice cream must use milk fat (dairy). A "frozen dessert" uses cheaper vegetable fat — often palm oil or coconut oil — as a substitute. Many popular products you've been buying for years are legally frozen desserts, not ice cream. The law requires manufacturers to declare which it is on the label — but in print so small, most people never notice. Before buying anything, flip the pack and look for the words "Ice Cream" or "Frozen Dessert" on the front or ingredient panel. This one check changes everything.

Why India's ice cream market is booming

The India ice cream market was valued at approximately ₹27,000–30,000 crore in 2026 and is growing at 15% annually — one of the fastest growth rates globally. The drivers are structural: rising disposable incomes, quick commerce platforms (Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart, Zepto can deliver in minutes), better cold chain infrastructure penetrating Tier 2 and 3 cities, and a young population that has grown up with global food references. Record milk output — India produced 239.3 million tonnes in 2023-24, up 63% from a decade ago — has also ensured reliable supply chains for dairy-based products.

Despite the boom, India's per capita consumption remains modest at 1.6 litres/year versus New Zealand's 28.4 litres. That gap is exactly the opportunity brands are chasing. The top six players — Amul, Kwality Wall's, Vadilal, Mother Dairy, Hatsun Agro, and Havmor/Creambell — control roughly 68–70% of the organised market. The rest is a fragmented landscape of regional champions, artisanal parlours, and international luxury brands.

🍓 Best Artisanal · Real Fruit Champion
Naturals Ice Cream
Kamaths Ourtimes Icecreams · Est. 1984 · Mumbai · ₹380 Cr brand

Naturals is the rarest thing in Indian retail: a 40-year-old brand that has never compromised its founding promise. Raghunandan Kamath launched it in 1984 with a radical idea — ice cream made from fresh fruit pulp, whole milk, and natural sugar. Nothing artificial. No preservatives. No synthetic colours. Four decades later, that's still exactly what they do.

The results speak for themselves. Their tender coconut ice cream made it onto Taste Atlas's list of top 100 iconic ice creams globally. The Sitaphal (custard apple) flavour has a cult following — people visit stores specifically to check if it's in season. Revenue jumped from ₹93 crore in FY21 to ₹380 crore in FY24, and the company targets ₹500 crore by FY27. By December 2025, they expanded to 200+ stores across 15 states.

The limitation is structural: because Naturals uses real seasonal fruit with no preservatives, they can't scale distribution like Amul. You mostly find them at their own outlets, not in general retail. But when you do find them, the experience is genuinely different from everything else in this list.

🥛 Best Real Dairy · Premium Indian Brand
Havmor
Lotte Confectionery (South Korea) · Est. 1944 · Gujarat origin

Havmor occupies a unique position in Indian ice cream: genuinely premium, genuinely dairy. Founded in 1944 (pre-partition, originally from Karachi), the brand was acquired by South Korea's Lotte Confectionery in 2017 and has since benefited from significant investment in quality and distribution. In 2025, Lotte completed a merger between Havmor Ice Cream and Lotte India (confectionery), creating a unified foods operation under the "One India" strategy.

Havmor's MD Komal Anand has been refreshingly direct about the market: "Most of the brands people think of as ice cream are actually frozen dessert players." Havmor isn't one of them. Their products use 100% dairy fat — real milk, real cream. The Choco Blast, Sitaphal, and Chikoo variants have a depth of flavour that you simply can't achieve with vegetable fat substitutes.

The brand's Havmor Restaurants (their own outlet chain) also serve full meals alongside ice cream — an unusual but effective retail format that creates a stronger brand experience than standard freezer placement in kirana stores.

One thing to note: like Amul, Vadilal's product range includes both real ice cream and frozen dessert products. Their premium variants tend to use dairy fat; the mass-market products may not. Check the label.

🏅 Widest Mass-Market Range
Kwality Wall's
The Magnum Ice Cream Company (formerly HUL) · Founded 1956 · Mumbai

Kwality Wall's is the brand behind some of India's most iconic frozen products — the Cornetto cone, the Feast chocolate bar, and the premium Magnum range. A familiar red and white logo, a heart on the packaging, and decades of consumer familiarity. In 2025, Hindustan Unilever spun off its ice cream business globally; Kwality Wall's now sits under The Magnum Ice Cream Company, Unilever's standalone ice cream entity.

Here's the honest truth that Kwality Wall's is less upfront about: most of their products — including the mass-market Feast bars, Cornetto cones, and family packs — are frozen desserts, not ice cream. They use vegetable fat. This is why Amul has run public awareness campaigns on the distinction, and why there have been legal disputes. The notable exception is the Magnum range, which uses dairy cream and qualifies as real ice cream — premium pricing, premium ingredients. In April 2026, Magnum launched Caramel Pop in India, a new premium variant.

For everyday indulgence without premium pricing, Kwality Wall's delivers. Cornetto is genuinely good eating. But if you care about dairy authenticity, the Magnum range is the only part of this portfolio that delivers real ice cream.

How to actually choose

1. Always check: Ice Cream vs Frozen Dessert

This is the single most important thing. Flip the pack and look for the declaration on the front or back. If it says "Frozen Dessert," it contains vegetable fat, not dairy fat. That's not automatically bad — it's still tasty — but it's not real ice cream, and you deserve to know what you're buying. Brands that use real dairy fat are legally required to say "Ice Cream."

2. Match your use case to the brand

  • Everyday treat at home: Amul or Mother Dairy (North), Arun (South)
  • Special occasion / gift: Haagen-Dazs or Baskin-Robbins
  • Real fruit, artisanal: Naturals Ice Cream — only from their outlets
  • Real dairy, premium daily: Havmor or Vadilal
  • Quick delivery at midnight: Creambell or Kwality Wall's via Blinkit

3. Seasonal flavours reward patience

The best Indian ice cream is seasonal. Naturals' Sitaphal (October–December) and Alphonso Mango (April–June) are as good as ice cream gets in this country. Vadilal's Alphonso variants peak in peak mango season. Missing these because you defaulted to chocolate chip year-round is a real loss — plan around the season.

4. For kids, budget is only one factor

  • Avoid very high-sugar novelty products with artificial colours for young children
  • Amul's simpler variants (cassatta, kesar pista cups) are better choices than neon-coloured novelties
  • Naturals' real fruit variants are the gold standard if you can access them
  • Single-ingredient options (vanilla with real cream, strawberry with real fruit) are best

Frequently asked

Which is the No. 1 ice cream brand in India?
Amul holds the #1 position by market share — approximately 12–21% of the organised market depending on how you measure it. With 150+ flavours, products from ₹10 to ₹500, and distribution reaching over a million retail outlets across India, no other brand comes close for volume or reach. That said, for quality, brands like Havmor (real dairy), Naturals (real fruit), and Haagen-Dazs (premium) offer experiences that exceed Amul in their respective niches.
What's the difference between ice cream and frozen dessert in India?
Under FSSAI (Food Safety and Standards Authority of India) regulations, "ice cream" must use dairy fat — milk fat from cow, buffalo, or goat milk. A "frozen dessert" replaces that dairy fat with vegetable fat, typically palm oil or coconut oil. Vegetable fat is cheaper, which is why so many brands use it. Both products are required to declare which they are on the label. Many Indians buy frozen desserts thinking they're getting ice cream. The label will clearly say "Ice Cream" or "Frozen Dessert" — check it every time.
Is Kwality Wall's real ice cream or frozen dessert?
The majority of Kwality Wall's products — Cornetto, Feast bar, most family packs — are frozen desserts, not ice cream. They use vegetable fat. Amul has run public awareness campaigns about this distinction, and there have been legal disputes. The exception within the Kwality Wall's portfolio is the Magnum range, which uses real dairy cream and qualifies as actual ice cream. If you're buying Kwality Wall's for the Magnum, you're getting real ice cream; if you're buying a Cornetto or Feast, you're getting frozen dessert.
Which Indian ice cream brand uses no artificial flavours or preservatives?
Naturals Ice Cream is the clearest answer. Founded in 1984 in Mumbai, Naturals has never used artificial flavours, preservatives, or artificial colours. Their ice cream is made from fresh fruit pulp, whole milk, and natural sugar only. Havmor also maintains strong standards for real dairy without artificial shortcuts. Haagen-Dazs, in the premium international category, uses no artificial flavours or preservatives in their products available in India.
Which is the best ice cream for South India?
Arun Ice Creams (part of Hatsun Agro Products) is the answer across Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Kerala. Founded in 1970 in Chennai, Arun has built unmatched distribution across South India at highly competitive prices starting from ₹5. Their Ibaco parlour brand offers a premium step-up experience for urban consumers. Outside South India, Arun has limited presence — but within its home region, it's the local champion that even Amul doesn't easily displace.
Is there a good vegan or dairy-free ice cream in India?
This is a small but growing segment. A few brands now offer plant-based options: NiceCream (Bengaluru-based, banana-based), Noto (cashew-based, growing in metros), and Minus 30 (offers some vegan options). Global brands like Ben & Jerry's occasionally stock vegan variants in premium Indian retail. Mass-market options remain limited in 2026, but demand is driving rapid product development, particularly in metro cities like Bengaluru, Mumbai, and Delhi.

The verdict

India's ice cream market is genuinely exciting right now — more innovation, better quality, and wider availability than at any point in history. Quick commerce has made late-night ice cream delivery a reality. Artisanal brands like Naturals are getting global recognition. Premium international brands are investing seriously in the Indian market.

But the frozen dessert issue remains the market's most frustrating quirk. Too many consumers are paying for "ice cream" and getting a vegetable-fat frozen dessert. That's not inherently wrong — frozen desserts taste good — but you deserve to make that choice knowingly. Read the label. Every time.

Beyond that: visit a Naturals outlet at least once this summer and order the Tender Coconut if it's in season. Try a Havmor scoop if you're in Gujarat or Maharashtra. Let Haagen-Dazs be your birthday splurge. And yes, keep that Amul Tricone in the freezer for Tuesday evenings when you just need something cold and good. All of these are valid choices — just make them with full information.
