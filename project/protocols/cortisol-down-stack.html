What this protocol is

The Cortisol-Down Stack · July 2026

Cortisol is not the enemy. It wakes you up, fights inflammation, and keeps you alive. The problem is cortisol staying high when it should be low — chronically, at night, under relentless stress. This stack targets that, at three different points.

"Lower your cortisol" has become wellness shorthand for "feel less stressed," and the internet now sells cortisol detoxes, cortisol face-fixes, and adrenal-fatigue cures. Most of it is nonsense. Cortisol is a normal, essential hormone that follows a daily rhythm — high in the morning to get you moving, low at night to let you sleep. You don't want to crush it; you want to restore the rhythm that chronic stress has flattened.

Three compounds do that at three genuinely different points in the stress system. KSM-66 ashwagandha turns down the HPA axis — the command line of stress — over weeks. L-theanine dampens the acute stress spike in minutes, without sedating you. Magnesium glycinate regulates the axis and protects the sleep that resets cortisol overnight. And because ashwagandha doesn't suit everyone, this protocol comes with an explicit plan B — exactly what to swap if you can't sleep on it.

Before you start — what cortisol is, and what actually moves it

Cortisol is your primary stress hormone, released by the adrenal glands under the control of the HPA (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal) axis. In a healthy rhythm it peaks about 30 minutes after waking and tapers to its lowest around midnight. Chronic stress — deadlines, poor sleep, over-training, financial worry — keeps the axis switched on, so cortisol runs high when it should be falling. That is the "tired but wired" state: exhausted but unable to switch off, poor sleep, and often stubborn abdominal fat, which cortisol promotes.

Here is the honest hierarchy, before any supplement: the biggest cortisol levers are behavioural. Consistent sleep, a caffeine cutoff (caffeine directly raises cortisol, and late caffeine wrecks the sleep that resets it), not living in an aggressive caloric deficit, training hard but not over-training, and actually reducing or reframing the stressors themselves — these move cortisol more than anything in a capsule. Supplements are a genuine adjunct for a high-stress phase, but they work on top of those fundamentals, not instead of them.

⚠
"Adrenal fatigue" is not a real diagnosis — be wary of anything selling a cure for it

A large category of products is marketed to fix "adrenal fatigue" — the idea that stress exhausts your adrenals until they can't produce cortisol. A systematic review found no scientific basis for the concept.9 Genuine adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease) is a real, serious, diagnosable condition that needs a doctor — not a supplement. This protocol is for chronically elevated cortisol from ordinary life stress, which is a completely different situation. If you have severe, persistent fatigue, unexplained weight changes, or dizziness, see a doctor rather than an adrenal-support blend.

1.KSM-66 Ashwagandha — the HPA-axis lever

Aw
Ashwagandha (KSM-66 root extract)
Strong evidence · direct cortisol reduction
300 mg KSM-66 twice daily (or 600 mg once) · with food · 8–12 week blocks

The headline compound, and one of the few adaptogens with genuinely strong human data for lowering cortisol. Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) acts on the HPA axis — the source of the stress response — reducing baseline cortisol output over weeks rather than producing an instant calm. It is also an Indian herb studied largely with an Indian-made extract, which makes it both authentic and easy to source here.

The evidence — real cortisol numbers

Chandrasekhar et al. (2012), a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in chronically stressed adults, gave 300mg of KSM-66 twice daily for 60 days and recorded a ~28% reduction in serum cortisol versus placebo, alongside large improvements in perceived-stress and anxiety scores.1 Salve et al. (2019) found dose-dependent cortisol reductions with KSM-66 at 240mg and 600mg,2 and Lopresti et al. (2019) reported lower morning cortisol and improved wellbeing on KSM-66 over 60 days.3 Across trials, the pattern is consistent — a meaningful drop in cortisol and stress scores, building over weeks. This is why ashwagandha, not theanine, is the compound doing the heavy lifting on baseline cortisol.

KSM-66 vs Sensoril vs churna — buy the standardised extract

The trials used standardised branded extracts, and so should you. KSM-66 is a root-only, high-concentration extract standardised to ~5% withanolides — it has the most cortisol and stress data and is the default for this protocol. Sensoril is a root-and-leaf extract standardised to a higher ~10% withanolides, usually dosed lower (125–250mg) and often experienced as more sedating — a reasonable pick if you want it mainly for evening calm. Plain ashwagandha churna from a kirana or Ayurvedic shop is cheap and traditional, but its withanolide content is unknown and variable, so you can't reproduce the studied dose. Pay for the standardised extract — it's the whole reason the effect is measurable.

⚠
Ashwagandha is not for everyone — the real cautions

Avoid ashwagandha in pregnancy and breastfeeding. Use caution if you have a thyroid condition or take thyroid medication (it can raise thyroid hormone levels), an autoimmune condition (it may stimulate immune activity), or take sedatives (additive effect). There are rare reports of liver injury — stop and see a doctor if you notice unusual fatigue, nausea, or yellowing of the skin/eyes. It is in the nightshade family. If you take any medication or have a health condition, clear it with a doctor before starting.

2.L-Theanine — the acute-spike lever

Th
L-Theanine
Moderate evidence · calm without sedation
200 mg · as needed before a stressor, or in the evening · fast-acting

The fast counterpart to ashwagandha's slow work. L-theanine is an amino acid found naturally in tea that produces "calm alertness" — it takes the edge off an acute stress response within 30–40 minutes, without making you drowsy or foggy. Where ashwagandha lowers your baseline over weeks, theanine is the compound you reach for before a stressful meeting, a difficult call, or when your mind won't settle at night.

How it works — alpha waves, not sedation

L-theanine increases alpha brain-wave activity, the electrical signature of a relaxed-but-awake state (think meditation, not sleep).6 Kimura et al. (2007) showed that theanine attenuated the physiological stress response — lower heart-rate rise and a blunted stress reaction — during an acute mental-stress task.4 Over four weeks, Hidese et al. (2019) found 200mg/day reduced stress-related symptoms and improved sleep quality in healthy adults.5 Crucially, it does this without sedation, so it can be used in the daytime — which is exactly why it occupies a different slot in this stack from the two evening compounds.

ℹ
Theanine is why chai feels calmer than coffee

Tea contains both caffeine and L-theanine, and the theanine smooths the caffeine's jitter — one reason a cup of chai feels steadier than the same caffeine from coffee. If you pair theanine with caffeine deliberately (about 200mg theanine to 100mg caffeine) you get focus without the wired edge, which the Desk Athlete Stack covers in detail. In this cortisol protocol, though, theanine's job is the opposite — taking the edge off, standalone, when stress spikes.

3.Magnesium Glycinate — the axis-and-sleep lever

Mg
Magnesium Glycinate
Moderate evidence · HPA regulation + sleep
200–400 mg elemental · evening · glycinate form

The third pathway, and the one that ties the stack to sleep. Magnesium is involved in regulating the HPA axis, and it has a bidirectional relationship with stress — stress depletes magnesium, and low magnesium amplifies the stress response, a genuine vicious circle. Taken in the evening as glycinate, it both helps break that circle and improves the deep sleep during which cortisol resets to its overnight low.

The magnesium-stress vicious circle

A 2020 review laid out the mechanism plainly: psychological stress increases urinary magnesium loss, and magnesium deficiency in turn heightens the body's susceptibility to stress and HPA-axis over-activation — each worsens the other.7 Since stress and low magnesium travel together, restoring magnesium status can lower the "gain" on the whole stress response. On top of that, magnesium supplementation improves measures of sleep quality,8 and the glycinate form pairs it with glycine — itself mildly calming — for a form that is well-absorbed, gentle on the gut, and well-suited to nightly evening use. This is the same compound the Wind-Down Protocol and Exam-Block Stack use; if you run one of those, you already have it — don't double up.

What to swap if you can't sleep on it

Here is the part most stack write-ups leave out. Ashwagandha is the strongest compound here, but it genuinely doesn't suit everyone — a minority find it subtly activating, get vivid or intense dreams, or simply sleep worse on it. If that's you, don't force it. Work through this in order.

IfAshwagandha leaves you wired or wakeful at night

Move it to the morning. For some people ashwagandha is mildly activating, so an evening dose backfires. Take your full dose (or both doses) earlier in the day — the cortisol-lowering effect is a baseline adaptation over weeks and does not depend on a bedtime dose.

IfYou get vivid or intense dreams

Halve the dose, or switch extract. Vivid dreams are a common, harmless report. Drop from 600mg to 300mg and see if it settles. If it persists, try switching between KSM-66 and Sensoril — people who react to one form often tolerate the other.

IfAshwagandha just doesn't agree with you at all

Drop it and lean on the other two pathways. Move L-theanine to bedtime (it supports sleep as well as calm), keep magnesium glycinate in the evening, and add 3g of glycine before bed — it has its own evidence for improving sleep quality and next-day alertness.10 This theanine + magnesium + glycine combination is a complete, ashwagandha-free calm-and-sleep stack.

IfYour real problem is sleep, not daytime stress

Run the sleep protocol instead. If the cortisol issue is downstream of poor sleep, the fix is upstream. The Wind-Down Protocol is built specifically for the Indian late-night schedule, with caffeine-cutoff tables and a low-dose melatonin window — often a better first move than a cortisol stack.

What we'd skip

The "cortisol" and "stress" aisle is full of products engineered for anxious buyers rather than for effect. These three are the ones we'd leave on the shelf.

Af
"Adrenal fatigue" cures & adrenal glandulars
Skip — treats a non-diagnosis

"Adrenal support" formulas and desiccated adrenal glandulars are sold to fix "adrenal fatigue" — a condition with no scientific basis.9 Worse, glandulars (made from ground-up animal adrenal tissue) can contain actual adrenal hormones, which is not something to self-dose blindly. If you genuinely suspect an adrenal problem, that is a reason to see an endocrinologist, not to buy a supplement built on a myth.

Verdict: you cannot treat a condition that doesn't exist. Real adrenal disease needs a doctor; ordinary stress needs the compounds above.
Cb
"Cortisol blocker" belly-fat pills
Skip — marketing, not medicine

The "cortisol blocker for stress belly fat" category is one of the oldest tricks in the supplement book — the original products in this space drew regulatory action for unsupported weight-loss claims, and nothing has changed the underlying reality. No pill "blocks" cortisol to melt abdominal fat. Cortisol-driven fat is addressed by fixing sleep, stress, and diet, not by a proprietary blend with a stock photo of a slim waistline on the bottle.

Verdict: if the pitch is "cortisol belly fat, gone," keep your wallet closed. It's a claim, not a mechanism.
Cm
Kitchen-sink "cortisol manager" blends
Skip — under-dosed

The "cortisol manager" or "stress complex" that lists ten calming ingredients — ashwagandha, theanine, magnesium, rhodiola, holy basil, phosphatidylserine, and more — almost always doses each one below what any trial used, to fit them all in two capsules. The likely result: a token amount of the ashwagandha that actually needed to be 600mg, and no clinical dose of anything. You are better off buying the three compounds in this protocol at their real, studied doses.

Verdict: a crowded label is a red flag. Dose three things properly rather than ten things pointlessly.

The bigger levers — the part that isn't a supplement

As with every protocol on this site, the honest finish: the biggest cortisol levers are free. If you do nothing else, these four outrank the three capsules above.

Lever 1

Protect your sleep

Sleep is when cortisol resets to its overnight low. Poor sleep keeps it elevated, which further wrecks sleep — the same vicious circle magnesium helps break. A consistent sleep and wake time does more for cortisol rhythm than any adaptogen.

Lever 2

Cut caffeine off early

Caffeine directly raises cortisol, and late caffeine sabotages the sleep that resets it. A hard early-afternoon cutoff is one of the most effective, and least popular, cortisol interventions there is.

Lever 3

Train — but don't over-train

Moderate exercise lowers stress and improves cortisol rhythm; chronic over-training does the opposite, keeping cortisol elevated. If you're stressed and grinding daily HIIT on poor sleep, more rest will lower cortisol more than another supplement.

Lever 4

Address the actual stressor

No supplement out-runs a genuinely unsustainable situation. Boundaries, workload, and the practices that regulate your nervous system — breathwork, time outdoors, real downtime — are the upstream fix the capsules only support.
